## 2. All variables that can make the weight wrong, and how we handle each

Columns: P = handled in the procedure (what the user does), PRE = handled by the program before detection,
POST = handled after detection (checks, warnings, model). "Measured" means we have our own number for it.

| # | Variable | How it makes the result wrong | How we handle it | Measured? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Camera height | Higher = seeds smaller in pixels = detector misses seeds | PRE: scale from the markers, resize to 0.110 mm/px (done) | Yes: sd 5.9 % to 4.0 %, correlation 0.63 to 0.04 |
| 2 | Phone resolution (MP) | Fewer pixels per seed, same as #1 | PRE: same fixed scale (done) | Yes: 12 MP -17.8 % to +0.1 %, 8 MP -42.6 % to +0.2 % |
| 3 | Phone model, lens, colour processing | Other sharpening, colours (warm cast on R&R photos) | P: main camera 1x, JPEG. Test: R&R + task 4 | Partly (task 4 and R&R to analyse) |
| 4 | Tilt of the phone | One side smaller than the other | P: level crosshair. POST: warning when the two markers differ (done). Possible: perspective correction from the marker corners | Yes: markers differ up to 3.6 % in one photo |
| 5 | Seeds touching | Detector merges or misses seeds | P: no seed touches. POST possible: warning when boxes overlap a lot | Yes: 17 to 36 % fewer seeds |
| 6 | Seeds close but not touching (dense spreading) | Some missed (P06) | P: spread wider. POST possible: density warning | Partly (P06 about 2 %) |
| 7 | Pile size per photo | Big piles do not fit or get packed denser | P: split into portions. POST: warn when count is above the tested range | Partly: 10 to 24 g tested; Jia up to 34 g (to analyse) |
| 8 | Other objects in the photo (notes, ruler, hands, clothes) | False seed boxes | P: only paper, seeds, markers. POST: count only `sunflower` boxes (done) | Yes: +2.5 % to +28.5 % with all boxes |
| 9 | Shadows | Dark areas may hide seeds or create false boxes | P: light from the side or above, phone not between lamp and seeds. Test on Jia's P10, P11, P13 | No (new) |
| 10 | Light and colour (daylight, lamp) | Contrast changes | P: same light in one session; R&R had other light | No (R&R and Jia give a first look) |
| 11 | Background | Contrast, texture | P: white matte paper | No (one background only) |
| 12 | Blur | Edges of seeds unclear | P: tap to focus. POST possible: sharpness check | No |
| 13 | Marker sheet print size | Wrong mm per pixel for every photo | P: check the 100 mm line once | Checked by Hadi |

Main message for the presentation: the variables with the biggest effect (height, phone, touching, other
objects) are already removed by measurement-based fixes, and we can show the before and after numbers.

## 3. The end user: how it should work

Goal: a farmer or technician does not need a ruler, a measured height or any settings.

What the user does (one card, 5 steps): put the marker sheet on white paper; pour the sample and spread it,
no seed touching; take the photo from where both markers and all seeds are in the picture; upload in the
app; read the weight with its range.

What the program does for the user (some done, some proposed):
- Done: finds the markers, fixes the scale, counts only seeds, gives weight with 95 % bounds, says "NO
  MARKER FOUND" or "tilted" with what to do.
- Proposed (small code, half a day each, estimate): a traffic light per photo with one plain sentence:
  green = OK; orange = "seeds too close together, spread them out" (box overlap or density check),
  "more seeds than we tested, split the sample" (count above the tested maximum), "photo not sharp";
  red = no marker. This turns every variable in section 2 into either an automatic fix or one clear
  instruction.
- Product idea (not for 26 Oct): print the markers in the corners of a larger sheet ("seed mat"). Then the
  user only has to fit the mat in the picture, the program crops to the mat (nothing else in the photo), and
  tilt can be corrected from the four corners.

## 4. Business case for the data

Two questions: why does CNH want seed weight from a photo, and how much data is worth collecting.

**Why (value)**: grams per seed is the same as the thousand-seed weight used in agriculture (our 0.0529 g per
seed would be about 53 g per 1000 seeds, our own measurement, one seed lot). A photo method replaces counting
and weighing by hand (for example for seed lots, sowing rate or harvest-loss checks). Jia prepares this
slide; any market or price figure must be looked up and sourced, not guessed.

**Cost of data (our own numbers)**: about 12 minutes per pile, one person (estimate from the 19 Oct session),
a kitchen scale, a printed sheet, a phone. 8 piles took about 1.5 hours; 15 piles about 3 hours (estimate).

**Value of one more pile**: the 95 % bound for a new sample is t x s x sqrt(1 + 1/n), with s = 4.0 %
(pile-to-pile spread, 8 piles). Computed in Python:

| Piles n | Bound (+-) | Time (12 min per pile, estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 19.9 % | 36 min |
| 5 | 12.2 % | 60 min |
| 8 | 10.0 % | 96 min |
| 10 | 9.5 % | 120 min |
| 15 | 8.9 % | 180 min |
| 30 | 8.3 % | 360 min |
| infinite | 7.8 % | |

Conclusion: after about 10 piles each extra pile buys less than 0.2 percentage points. The bound can never
go below about 7.8 % by collecting more of the same data, because that floor is the natural spread between
piles. **Stop rule**: stop collecting when one more sample improves the bound less than a set amount, or
when the model is no longer the largest error source. To go below the floor you must reduce the spread
itself (a 0.01 g scale, a better detector for dense seeds), not collect more. **When to collect again**: new
seed lot or variety, new phone type, other background or light, or when the traffic light shows many
orange photos in use.

Error budget (all 17 piles, relative standard errors): total 3.97 %; balance 1.73 % (RMS); photo
repeatability 1.33 % for one photo (0.94 % for the mean of 2 photos, as the piles are); left over 3.32 %
(sqrt(3.97^2 - 1.73^2 - 1.33^2), unrounded inputs): seed weight plus unexplained detector and weighing
error. So **the photo is no longer the main error**: what is left sits in the seeds, the scale and weighing.

## 4. The end user

Rule: every variable is either fixed by the program, or turned into one simple instruction on the screen.

The user does 5 steps: marker sheet on white paper; pour and spread the seeds (none touching); photo from
any height with both markers and all seeds in it; upload; read "17.3 g (15.6 to 19.0 g)".

**Traffic light** (proposed): a colour next to each result.
- Green: all checks passed, use the result.
- Orange: a result is shown, but one check failed; one sentence says how to fix it ("Seeds too close
  together: spread them", "More seeds than we tested: split the sample", "Shadow on the seeds", "Other
  objects in the photo", "Photo not sharp").
- Red: no result ("No marker found: both markers must be fully in the photo", "Phone tilted").
Each check is one variable from section 3, with its limit taken from our own photos (for example the box
overlap in our touching photos versus our normal photos).

## 5. Business case for the data

Measured time per pile (first photo of one pile to the first of the next, includes weighing and
spreading): 12.7 min (Hadi, 10-24 g), 10.8 min (Jia task 4, 11-23 g), 21.6 min (Jia, 26-34 g). All our
photo sessions together: 4.8 hours. Example labour cost: 48.2 euro per hour (Eurostat, Belgium, 2024
average hourly labour cost), so about 10 euro per small pile and 17 euro per large pile (example).

What one more pile buys (bound = t x s x sqrt(1 + 1/n), with s = 3.97 %, our pooled spread):

| Target bound | Piles needed | Time (12.7 min per pile) | Cost (example rate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| +-12 % | 6 | 1.3 h | 61 euro |
| +-10 % | 8 | 1.7 h | 82 euro |
| +-9 % | 13 | 2.8 h | 133 euro |
| +-8.75 % | 16 | 3.4 h | 163 euro |
| +-8.5 % | 21 | 4.4 h | 214 euro |
| +-8 % | 63 | 13.3 h | 643 euro |

With this setup it can never go below +-7.8 % (1.96 x 3.97 %), however many piles are added. This floor
belongs to our setup (1 g kitchen scale, one seed lot), not to the method: without the scale-step error it
would be about +-7.0 % (estimate). Each pile after the 10th improves the bound by less than 0.2 percentage
points on average; after the 17th by less than 0.07. Caveat: the table treats the spread 3.97 % as known;
with 17 piles the true spread could be 0.74 to 1.52 times that (95 %).

**Stop rule**: stop when one more pile improves the bound by less than about 0.2 percentage points (about
10 to 12 piles), or when the photo is no longer the biggest error. We are past both: 17 piles, and the
photo error (1.3 %) is much smaller than the rest (3.3 %). To do better, spend the money on the
largest error instead: a 0.01 g scale and single-seed weighings, or calibrating per seed lot.
**Collect again** when the seed lot or variety changes, or when many photos in use get an orange light.
